<objective>
Give an office administrator the three operations the matrix screen cannot express: create a role,
rename a role, delete a role — each scoped to their own tenant, each refusing what PAPEL-05 and
PAPEL-08 say must be refused.

These land in a **new** controller, `OfficeRolesController` at `/api/v1/admin/rbac/roles`, rather
than in `AdminController`. The reason is structural safety, not tidiness: plan 02 changed
`AdminController`'s class-level gate to `hasAuthority('users:manage')`, so a role-CRUD handler added
there would silently inherit the wrong authority if anyone ever forgot its method-level override. A
dedicated class with a **class-level** `hasAuthority('rbac:manage')` gate cannot be got wrong by
omission, and it mirrors how `PlatformAdminController` is already organised — one class, one
authority. `AdminController` is also already ~560 lines, and this codebase has one cautionary example
of what happens when a controller keeps absorbing unrelated CRUD.

Purpose: PAPEL-02, PAPEL-04 and PAPEL-05 become real, with PAPEL-08's undeletable-administrator rule
keyed on provenance rather than on a name that is now editable.
Output: three endpoints, four refusals proven by test, zero schema change.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| client → `/api/v1/admin/rbac/roles/**` | an office administrator submits role names, permission key lists and role ids |
| office tenant A → office tenant B | every handler resolves ids and names only within `principal.getTenantId()` |
| office surface → `PLATAFORMA_ADMIN` | a name or a provenance that would reach the platform role must be unreachable from here |
